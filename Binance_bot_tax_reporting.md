# Binance Bot Trading Tax Reporting Guide

This guide explains how to generate, read, and use the Binance Bot tax reports generated by CrypTax to declare your cryptocurrency bot trading activity (such as Grid or Spot bots) to your local tax authorities.

---

## 1. How to Generate Your Bot Tax Report

CrypTax automatically consolidates your Binance API transactions and your bot trading spreadsheets into a single unified report. Follow these steps:

### Step 1: Export Your Bot Activity from Binance
1. Log into your Binance account on the web.
2. Navigate to your **Trading Bots** history page.
3. Export your trade history as a **CSV file** (ensure it contains columns like `Time`, `Side`, `Strategy_Id`, `Order Amount`, and `Trading total`).
   * *Note: If you have run bots across multiple periods, you can upload multiple CSV files; CrypTax will automatically merge and deduplicate them chronologically.*

### Step 2: Upload and Generate the Report in CrypTax
1. Open the **CrypTax Web Interface**.
2. Start a new report generation job for the relevant tax year.
3. In the upload section, upload your exported Binance Bot CSV file(s) into the **Bot Activity** field.
4. Fill in your Binance API credentials (so CrypTax can fetch your standard trades, deposits, and withdrawals).
5. Click **Generate Report**.

Once the job is completed, you will receive a confirmation email with a ZIP file containing your final reports.

---

## 2. Understanding Your Bot Report Spreadsheet

Your email will contain a file named `Binance_[Year]_[username]_bot_report_out.ods`. Open this file in Microsoft Excel or LibreOffice. It contains four tabs:

### Tab 1: `Bot Activity Summary`
* **What it is:** A high-level overview of each individual bot instance (grouped by its unique `Strategy_Id`).
* **Use case:** Great for your own analysis to see which specific bot strategy performed best.

### Tab 2: `Closed Operations`
* **What it is:** The detailed buy/sell matching of trades on a **per-bot basis** (treating each bot instance in isolation).

### Tab 3: `Pair Activity Summary` (Hacienda / Tax Compliant)
* **What it is:** A high-level summary of your trading **grouped by coin pair** (e.g., `SOLUSDC` or `BNBUSDC`), completely ignoring bot boundaries. 
* **Use case:** This is the sheet you must use to fill out your tax return.

### Tab 4: `Pair Closed Operations` (Hacienda / Tax Compliant)
* **What it is:** The chronological FIFO (First-In, First-Out) matching of all bot trades pooled together for each pair, applying the **accurate daily historical exchange rate** to convert your transactions to your local currency (e.g., EUR).
* **Use case:** This is your audit trail. Keep this sheet safe as proof for tax authorities in case of an audit.

## 3. What to Report to Tax Authorities (FIFO vs. Per-Bot)

When you trade using grid or spot bots, it is tempting to calculate your gains on a per-bot basis. However, **tax regulations in almost all countries (including Spain) do not recognize "bots" as separate tax entities.**

### Why You Must Use the `Pair` Tabs:
* **The Chronological Queue:** Tax authorities require you to merge all transactions of a cryptocurrency (`SOL`) inside your exchange account chronologically. 
* **Global FIFO:** A sale from `Bot B` in July might be matched with a buy from `Bot A` in March if it was the earliest available buy in your account. You cannot legally isolate bot activity into individual silos to manipulate or realize losses earlier.
* **Accurate Exchange Rates:** The `Pair` tabs in this report use the correct daily exchange rate for stablecoins (e.g., converting `USDC` to `EUR` on the exact day of the trade), preventing you from overpaying taxes compared to standard tools that simplify exchange rates to 1:1.

## 4. Practical Example: Reporting in Spain (Hacienda)

If you are a Spanish tax resident reporting your bot gains to **Hacienda**, you will declare your trading profits under the **Gains and Losses from Capital Assets** (*Ganancias y Pérdidas Patrimoniales*) in your **Modelo 100** (Renta) tax return.

### Step-by-Step Reporting in Modelo 100:

1. **Locate your figures:**
   * Open the **`Pair Activity Summary`** tab.
   * For each pair (e.g., `SOLUSDC`), look at the **`pl_local`** column. This is your net taxable profit in **EUR**.
   * Look at the **`base_vol_sell`** (converted to EUR) as your total Sale Value, and the **`base_vol_buy`** (converted to EUR) as your total Acquisition Value.

2. **Fill in the Modelo 100 form (Gains section):**
   * Select the asset type: **Other capital assets / shares** (*Monedas virtuales*).
   * **Acquisition Value (Valor de adquisición):** Enter the total cost of the purchases (from the `base_vol_buy` column converted to EUR).
   * **Transmission/Sale Value (Valor de transmisión):** Enter the total revenue from the sales (from the `base_vol_sell` column converted to EUR).
   * **Net Gain/Loss:** The form will automatically calculate your net taxable profit, which should match the sum of **`pl_local`** in your unified pair sheets (e.g., **`624.53 EUR`** for SOL).

3. **Keep Your Proof:**
   * Download and save the **`Pair Closed Operations`** sheet.
   * If *Hacienda* requests an audit or asks to verify your calculations, this sheet serves as the official, line-by-line FIFO matching proof demonstrating exactly how every single fraction of a coin was acquired and sold at the official ECB exchange rates.
